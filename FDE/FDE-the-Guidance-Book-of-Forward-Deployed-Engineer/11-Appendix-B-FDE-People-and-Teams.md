# Appendix B · FDE People and Teams

This appendix lists the people, teams, and sources of knowledge that are indispensable to understanding the FDE model. For the complete list of sources consulted while writing this book, see the research notes published alongside the manuscript.

## I. Key People

**Shyam Sankar** — President and Chief Technology Officer of Palantir, and an early employee. Widely regarded as the inventor of the FDE strategy: the person who redefined "on-site customization" from a cost into "product discovery." His public statements are first-hand material for understanding Palantir's philosophy.

**Bob McGrew** — Early engineer at PayPal, early executive at Palantir, and former Chief Research Officer of OpenAI (led the development of ChatGPT, GPT-4, and o1). The best explicator of the FDE model. "The gravel road and the highway" and "doing the unscalable at scale" both come from him. Must-read: the YC Lightcone podcast "The FDE Playbook for AI Startups" (September 2025).

**Stephen Cohen** — Co-founder of Palantir. Inventor of the demo loop: the protagonist of "this thing is terrible" and "so what would you like it to be different?"

**Alex Karp** — CEO of Palantir. Originator of the "French waiter" behavioral model: opposing a yes-man, order-taking engineering culture.

**Barry** — Former Palantir Forward Deployed Engineer and author of "Understanding Forward Deployed Engineering." The single most important "insider's warning" on the FDE model: a true ledger of cost, chaos, and burnout.

**Ted Mabrey** — Palantir veteran and writer, and first-hand recollector of the "Sankar bomb" email (the all-hands message in which Sankar announced the strategic pivot). He broke "let results speak for everything" into two questions: "Does it actually work? Does it actually matter?"

**Nabeel Qureshi** — Spent nearly eight years as a Forward Deployed Engineer at Palantir, and author of "Reflections on Palantir" (2024). Embedded on-site at Airbus in Toulouse for a year; "to hell with generalizability," the "bat signal" reverse-screening approach to hiring, "ninety-five percent of data problems are ingestion, cleaning, and joining," and the moral triage of "staying in the room" when facing gray-area projects — all drawn from his public writing.

**Vinoo Ganesh** — Designer of Palantir's rotational training program (Project Frontline), and author of "The Definitive Guide to FDE" (2026). "You can't build a real FDE organization by hiring — you can only grow one" — over 250 engineers rotated through into real deployments, with alumni scattered across OpenAI, xAI, and Anduril. The six-month self-directed cultivation checklist in Chapter 7 and the "2.3 million data entries blew out the memory" incident postmortem in Chapter 4 both come from his first-hand writing.

**Colin Jarvis** — Head of OpenAI's Forward Deployed Engineering team, the person who built OpenAI's FDE organization from the ground up.

**Brad Lightcap** — OpenAI's Chief Operating Officer, and the driver of the "deployment company."

**Natalie Meurer** — Head of agent engineering at Sierra (five years at Palantir prior). Coined the term "agent engineer." Must-read: Latent Space podcast interview (July 2026).

**Bret Taylor / Clay Bavor** — Founders of Sierra (the former was formerly co-CEO of Salesforce, the latter formerly a vice president at Google). The people who turned the FDE model into a business model itself (charging by results).

**Jesse Zhang / Ashwin Sreenivas** — Founders of Decagon (the latter came from Palantir). Designers of agentic operating procedures, and a template for "productizing delivery experience."

**David Wakeling** — Former head of market innovation at the law firm Linklaters, and the in-firm champion of Harvey's first lighthouse deployment. The best exemplar of the client-side supporter's perspective.

## II. Signature Teams

**Palantir FDE Organization** — Inventor and finished form of the model. The "Echo–Delta–Platform" triangle, the bootcamp machine.

**OpenAI Forward Deployed Engineering Team** — Assembled in 2024, globally distributed; the operators behind John Deere and Banco Santander; upgraded into a "deployment company" in 2026.

**Anthropic Applied AI Team** — An FDE establishment organized under the name "Applied AI Engineer," co-builder of FIS's financial-crime agent; in 2026 formed an enterprise AI services joint venture with Blackstone and others.

**Sierra Agent Engineering Team** — The extreme form of charging by results plus managed delivery (founded 2023, valued at $4.5 billion in October 2024, and reportedly climbing since).

**Harvey Deployment Team** — The textbook for vertical-industry (legal) FDE (founded 2022, valued at $11 billion in March 2026, with users spanning 100,000+ lawyers).

**Cognition Forward Deployed Team** — "Embedded inside the partner's body" as the model for ecosystem bundling: starting in 2026, an embedded Forward Deployed Engineer team is permanently stationed at Cognizant, the global IT services giant, responsible for project screening, engineer mentoring, and outcome measurement — leveraging the client relationships the partner accumulated over decades to push the product into large enterprises in healthcare, finance, and insurance.

**Scale AI / Databricks / Salesforce / Google Cloud / Mistral / Cohere** — Each maintains FDE or analogous roles, under different names (solutions architect, customer engineer, etc.), but with the same skeleton.

**Volcano Engine Doubao LLM FDE Team (火山引擎豆包大模型 FDE 团队)** — The clearest FDE establishment among China's big internet companies (founded 2026, covering automotive, healthcare, education, finance, and semiconductors; co-built a thousand-person team with Ernst & Young).

**Chinese indigenous FDE pioneers: Qimeng Technology (启盟科技), Quantuo Technology (钱拓科技)** — Local service providers staked out in real-estate facilities management and finance, who publicly produced the definitional thinking that "FDE is not equal to on-site outsourcing."

## III. Knowledge Sources (Ranked by Importance)

1. YC Lightcone podcast: "The FDE Playbook for AI Startups — Bob McGrew Interview" (September 2025) — a 51-minute debrief from the model's first storyteller.
2. a16z: "Trading Margin for Moat" — the benchmark for business-model analysis.
3. Barry: "Understanding Forward Deployed Engineering" — a calm insider's warning.
4. MIT's NANDA Lab: "The GenAI Divide: The State of Business AI in 2025" (The GenAI Divide) — the source of the "95% failure rate," the data foundation for FDE's reason to exist.
5. The New Stack: "Why OpenAI and Anthropic Are Both Racing to Build FDE Teams" (May 2026).
6. CIO.com: "Anthropic's Financial Agent Exposes a New Bottleneck: the Forward Deployed Engineer" (May 2026) — the buyer's perspective and a Gartner warning.
7. Latent Space podcast: "Forward Deployed Engineers and the Future of Software Engineering" (July 2026) — the Sierra perspective.
8. Palantir official blog: "A Day in the Life of a Palantir Forward Deployed Engineer" (2020) — official self-account, with a Chinese translation circulating.
9. OpenFDE community (open-fde.com) — an open-source community of practitioners; a map of roles and companies.
10. 36Kr's "China's To B Software, Out of the Trap of Loss-Leading Growth," and Chen Guo (George)'s WeChat-account FDE series — essential Chinese-context counterpoints.

## IV. Compensation and Job-Search References

- GetPerspective: "2026 Forward Deployed Engineer Compensation Report" (1,200 samples) — for top-tier lab mid-level FDEs, median total annual compensation is about $385,000, senior about $610,000, and principal over $1.2 million.
- fdenest.com / fde.academy / sundeepteki.org — three specialist sites for interview prep and career paths.
- Levels.fyi — real-time compensation data for roles at Palantir and others.
- The job postings themselves are the best textbook: the FDE postings from OpenAI, Anthropic, Decagon, Harvey, and Scale AI are worth studying word for word.
